# Escalation

**Not ingested into the knowledge base.** See `README.md` in this directory for why.

The staff knowledge base deliberately stops short and points here. These are the cases where a
moderator or reviewer should not decide alone.

## Escalate, do not decide, when

| Situation | Why |
|---|---|
| Suspected forged or altered verification document | Fraud, and possibly criminal. A rejection reason that describes what you noticed teaches the next attempt |
| A mission suggests an offence is being planned or is under way | Rejecting closes the mission and ends our visibility of it |
| A request appears aimed at a person protected by an order | Legal exposure, and a safety matter |
| A pattern across accounts, missions or disputes | One case is a decision; a pattern is a different question |
| Any law-enforcement or regulator contact | See `law-enforcement-requests.md` |
| A decision you would not want reviewed | The reliable signal that it needs a second person |

## How to escalate

1. **Do not act on the underlying decision.** Leave the mission held or the account as-is.
2. **Do not tell the subject what you noticed.** A rejection reason that describes the detection
   is a tutorial for the next attempt. Use a neutral reason, or none, and note internally.
3. Record what you saw, with references — mission, account, document, evidence **IDs, never
   content**.
4. Route to the escalation owner for that category.
5. Stop. Do not investigate further yourself.

## Suspected document fraud

Verification review only. Do not approve, and do not reject with a reason naming the
indicator.

Escalate with: the verification reference, which document, and what you observed. The applicant
is told their application is under review — nothing more.

## Suspected planned offence

Where a mission suggests harm to a person, escalate rather than reject. A rejected mission is
gone; an escalated one is preserved with its content and its author.

Where we are legally required to report, that obligation sits with the escalation owner, not
with the reviewer who spotted it.

## Pattern detection

Resolve the case in front of you on its own merits. Then escalate the pattern separately, with
the cases that make it up.

Do not adjust an individual decision because of a suspected pattern — that makes the individual
decision unsound and the pattern unprovable.
