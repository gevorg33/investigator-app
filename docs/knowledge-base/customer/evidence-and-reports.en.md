# Evidence and reports

## What will I actually receive?

The deliverables listed in the quote you accepted. Typically a written report, plus the
evidence items supporting it — photographs, video, documents or records, depending on the
work.

The quote states the deliverables. If something you expected is not listed there, it was
not agreed, and it is better to establish that before accepting than after.

## What is in the report?

A structured document: a summary, the findings, the methodology used, the limitations of
what could be determined, and any attachments. The investigator signs it before it is
released to you.

The limitations section matters. A report that cannot establish something will say so.
That is a sign the report is honest, not that the work was inadequate.

## How do I access my evidence and reports?

Through the assignment in the app. Evidence is private by default and is released to you
when the investigator makes it available.

There are no permanent public links to evidence. Each time you open an item, the platform
issues a short-lived, authorized link for you specifically. Links expire, which is why a
link copied and saved will stop working.

## Can I download and keep my evidence and reports?

Yes, within the terms of your assignment. Exported files are watermarked with who exported
them and when.

Once a file is on your device, protecting it is your responsibility. Evidence often
contains personal information about identifiable people, and you remain subject to the law
in how you use and store it.

## Can I see where the investigator's information came from?

You see the sources your investigator chooses to share with you, and the findings in your report
say what they rest on. An investigator does not have to share every source while the work is under
way, and some — a witness, for example — may stay private to protect the person concerned.

A source can be withdrawn by the investigator if it turns out to be wrong or irrelevant. It then
disappears from your view but is kept, so the record of the work stays complete.

## Who else can see my evidence?

You and the assigned investigator. Nobody else has automatic access, including our staff.

Staff can access evidence only when there is a specific reason — most often a dispute you
raised — and that access requires an explicit grant, is recorded, and is visible to you.
There is no mode in which staff simply browse customer evidence.

## Can I share evidence with my lawyer or with someone else?

You can download it and share it yourself, subject to your assignment terms and the law.

There is no in-platform sharing to people outside the assignment. If you need someone else
to have access inside the platform, that is a support request, not something you can grant
yourself — the access model deliberately does not allow customers to widen access.

## Why can't I edit or delete an evidence item?

Because evidence that can be altered after the fact is worth much less. Each item records
who uploaded it, when, and a checksum that proves the file has not changed since.

If an item is wrong, the investigator submits a corrected version linked to the original.
Both are retained. The same principle applies to reports: revisions create new versions
rather than overwriting.

## The report says something I disagree with. What can I do?

Request a revision. That returns it to the investigator with your comments, and they can
respond or produce a new version.

If the disagreement is about a conclusion the evidence does not support, say so
specifically — which finding, and why. A well-specified revision request is far more likely
to resolve than a general objection. If it cannot be resolved, raise a dispute.

## How long is my evidence kept?

Retention periods depend on the type of data and the jurisdiction, and are set out in the
retention policy.

Evidence is not kept indefinitely. If you need to retain something beyond the retention
period, download it before then.
