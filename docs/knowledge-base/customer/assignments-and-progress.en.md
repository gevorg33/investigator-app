# Assignments and progress

## What is an assignment?

An assignment is the actual agreement between you and one investigator, created when you
accept a quote and payment is authorized. It fixes the scope, the price, the deadlines, who
may see the evidence and reports, and the cancellation and dispute terms.

One mission produces one assignment. If you need two investigators, you need two missions.

## What do the different statuses mean?

| Status | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Draft | Your mission is not yet submitted. Only you can see it. |
| Submitted / Under review | Being checked before investigators see it. |
| Quoted | Investigators have sent quotes. Your move. |
| Confirmed / Paid | You accepted a quote; payment is being authorized. |
| Assigned / Accepted | The investigator has taken the work on. |
| In progress | Work is under way. |
| Report submitted | The investigator has delivered their report. |
| Customer review | Your turn to review and accept, or request revisions. |
| Completed | Finished and closed. You can leave a review. |
| Cancelled / Rejected / Expired | Closed without an assignment being completed. |
| Disputed | Under staff review after you raised a dispute. |

## How do I know work is actually happening?

The investigator updates the assignment as they go, and you are notified when something
changes — work starting, evidence being uploaded, a report being submitted.

Investigation work is not continuous, and quiet periods are normal. If you have heard
nothing and the timeframe concerns you, message the investigator through the assignment
first. That conversation is on record, which matters if it later becomes a dispute.

## How long will my investigation take?

The estimated duration is in the quote you accepted. It is an estimate made by that
investigator for that scope, not a platform-wide timeframe, and it varies enormously
between types of work.

If the work is going to take longer than estimated, the investigator should tell you. A
materially longer timeframe than agreed is something you can raise as a revision request or
a dispute.

## Can I change the scope after work has started?

Not unilaterally. The assignment fixes what was agreed, and the investigator priced their
work against it.

Discuss it with the investigator. If you both agree on a different scope, that is a new
quote — either an additional one for the extra work, or a replacement if the direction has
changed substantially.

## Can I cancel after the investigator has started?

You can, but the cancellation terms in your accepted quote determine what you are refunded.
Work already done is generally chargeable.

If you are cancelling because something has gone wrong rather than because you changed your
mind, raise a dispute instead — that route can result in a refund that a plain cancellation
would not.

## What if the investigator stops responding?

Message them through the assignment so there is a record, then contact support if there is
still no response. Do not chase them outside the platform.

Staff can see the assignment's history and messages, and can step in — including
reassigning or refunding where an investigator has abandoned work.

## Can I work with the same investigator again?

Yes. Create a new mission and ask them to quote on it. Each piece of work is a separate
mission, assignment and payment, with its own scope and its own evidence.

There is no ongoing retainer arrangement in the platform. Continuing work is a series of
missions.
