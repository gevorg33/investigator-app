# Revisions and disputes

## What is the difference between a revision and a dispute?

A revision asks the investigator to fix or add something. It stays between the two of you
and is the right first step in most cases.

A dispute brings staff in to review what was agreed against what was delivered. It is the
route when a revision has not worked, or when the problem is not something a revision can
fix.

## How do I request a revision?

From the assignment, while it is in customer review. Say specifically what is wrong: which
finding, which piece of evidence, or what part of the agreed scope is missing.

"This is not what I wanted" gives the investigator nothing to act on. "The report covers
the business address but the quote included the second site, which is not mentioned" does.

## How many revisions can I request?

That depends on the terms in the quote you accepted. Some investigators include a set
number; others handle it case by case.

A revision that asks for work outside the agreed scope is a new quote, not a revision, and
the investigator is entitled to say so.

## When should I raise a dispute?

When the work delivered does not match what was agreed and a revision has not resolved it.
Common grounds: agreed deliverables missing, work not carried out, findings the evidence
does not support, or missed deadlines that change the value of the work.

Disagreeing with a conclusion is not by itself grounds. An investigation that honestly
reports it could not establish something has done its job — the limitations section exists
for exactly that.

## What happens when I raise a dispute?

The assignment pauses and staff review it: the mission, the accepted quote, the messages,
the evidence and the report. Both you and the investigator can provide context.

Staff decide on the basis of what was agreed and what was delivered, and the decision is
recorded with its reason. Outcomes can include a required revision, a partial refund, or a
full refund.

## How long does a dispute take?

It depends on the complexity and on how quickly both parties respond. The single biggest
factor you control is how clearly you set out the problem at the start.

Attach or point to specifics: the clause in the quote, the message where something was
agreed, the finding you are challenging.

## Can I leave a review if I was unhappy?

Yes, once the assignment is completed. Reviews are part of how other customers judge
investigators.

A review is not a substitute for a dispute — it does not get you a refund. If you want
money back, raise the dispute.

## What if I am unhappy with the dispute outcome?

Contact support and say why, including anything not considered the first time. Decisions
are recorded with their reasoning, so it can be reviewed.

The platform's dispute process does not remove any legal rights you have. If the matter is
serious enough, you retain whatever recourse the law in your jurisdiction provides.
