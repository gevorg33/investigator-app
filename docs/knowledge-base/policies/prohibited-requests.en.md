# Prohibited requests

> Plain-language summary of the lawful use policy. The authoritative terms are the legal
> policy pages, which take precedence if the two ever differ.

## What kinds of work are not allowed?

Anything unlawful. In particular, the platform does not support:

- Gaining access to accounts, devices, systems or networks that are not yours
- Installing or using spyware, stalkerware, or any covert monitoring software
- Tracking a person's location without a lawful basis
- Recording people where doing so is unlawful
- Entering property unlawfully
- Harassment, intimidation, or stalking
- Obtaining private records — financial, medical, telecommunications — without lawful authority
- Work whose purpose is to enable any of the above

This is not the complete list. A request can be refused because it is unlawful even if it
is not named here.

## Why was my request refused when I believe it is legal?

Usually because of how it was described rather than what you intended. A request to
"find out where someone is staying and when they leave the house" reads as surveillance of
a private individual regardless of your reason for asking.

If your purpose is lawful, describe it: who you are in relation to the subject, what
decision the information supports, and what legal basis you have. A reviewer can act on
that. If a mission was refused and you think the refusal misread your request, revise it
with that context or contact support.

## Can I find out where someone lives?

Not as a general request. Locating a private individual is only supportable where there is
a lawful basis — for example, serving legal documents, enforcing a judgment, or a matter
where you hold a recognised legal interest.

You will be asked what that basis is. "I want to know where they are" is not one, and
missions of that shape are rejected.

## Can I have someone followed?

Surveillance is lawful in some contexts and unlawful in others, and it depends on where it
happens, who the subject is, and why. Investigators licensed in your jurisdiction know
those limits and will decline work that crosses them.

A mission that asks for surveillance without a stated lawful purpose is refused. One with a
clear basis may be accepted, and the investigator remains responsible for working within
the law.

## Can I ask for someone's phone records, bank records or medical records?

No. These are protected in essentially every jurisdiction the platform operates in, and
obtaining them without authority is a criminal offence for both the requester and the
investigator.

An investigator can work with records you are lawfully entitled to obtain, or that are
publicly available. They cannot obtain protected records on your behalf.

## What happens if I submit a prohibited request?

The mission is rejected and the reason is recorded. A single rejected mission does not
affect your account.

Repeated attempts, or an attempt to disguise a prohibited request as something else, can
result in suspension. Where we are legally required to report a request, we will.

## Does the assistant decide whether my request is allowed?

No. The assistant can tell you that a request looks like it falls into a prohibited
category, and it can help you describe a lawful request clearly. It does not make the
decision.

Policy decisions are made by deterministic checks and, where needed, by trained staff. The
outcome is recorded with its reason.
