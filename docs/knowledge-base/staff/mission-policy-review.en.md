# Mission policy review

## What am I deciding?

Whether a mission that was held can be released to investigators, needs more information
from the customer, or must be rejected.

The automatic checks decide the clear cases. What reaches you is what needs a person.

## What is the test?

Whether the work requested can be carried out lawfully. Not whether it is unusual, morally
uncomfortable, or likely to be difficult — those are not grounds.

Many lawful missions read badly because customers describe them poorly. Locating a person
sounds like stalking until you know the customer is enforcing a judgment.

## When should I ask for more information rather than rejecting?

Whenever the lawfulness turns on something the customer has not said — their relationship to
the subject, the decision the information supports, or the legal basis they hold.

Asking is usually the right first step. A rejection that was really a request for context
produces a resubmission of the same mission and teaches the customer nothing.

## What reason do I record for a rejection?

The specific policy ground, in terms the customer can understand and act on. The reason is
stored and shown to them.

Do not describe what triggered the automated flag. Explain the policy position, not the
detection.

## Can I approve a mission narrowed to its lawful part?

No. You approve or reject the mission as written, or ask for changes.

Silently releasing a narrowed reading leaves investigators quoting against one interpretation
and the customer expecting another, which becomes a dispute.

## What if the mission suggests an offence is being planned?

Escalate rather than simply rejecting. A rejection closes the mission and ends the platform's
visibility of it.

The escalation path, including when the platform contacts authorities, is in the internal
operations handbook, not here.

## What if I am unsure?

Escalate. An uncertain approval carries far more risk than a delay, and the decision you make
is attributed to you and reviewable afterwards.

There is no expectation that you resolve every case alone.

## Does the AI classification decide anything?

No. It flags and it can summarise, and its classification is visible to you as input.

The decision is yours and is recorded as yours. Do not record the assistant's assessment as
the reason for your decision.

## Can a customer appeal?

They can revise and resubmit, or contact support. Support can ask for a decision to be
reviewed.

Because decisions are recorded with their reasoning, a review looks at what you actually
considered — which is the reason to write the reason properly at the time.
