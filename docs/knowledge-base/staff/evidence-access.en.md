# Evidence access

## When may I access customer evidence?

Only when a specific task requires it — most often a dispute you are handling, or an abuse
report that turns on the content.

There is no general browsing access, and there is no administrative override. Curiosity,
quality sampling and "checking how it's going" are not reasons.

## How does access work?

It requires an explicit grant tied to the reason. The grant is recorded, time-bounded, and
visible to the customer in their own access log.

The customer sees that a staff member opened their evidence and when. This is deliberate:
their evidence is private, and being told who looked at it is part of what private means.

## Can I download evidence?

No. Review it in the platform. Do not download, screenshot, forward, or store it anywhere
else.

Evidence often contains identifiable people who never consented to anything and are not
parties to the assignment. It stays where its retention rule and access controls apply.

## Do I need access to resolve a dispute?

Usually not. Most disputes are decided on the quote against the report and the assignment
record.

Open evidence when the decision genuinely depends on its content — whether a deliverable
exists, or whether a finding is supported. Not to form a general impression.

## What if I open something I should not have?

Say so, promptly. The access is recorded whether or not you report it, and a self-reported
mistake is treated very differently from one found in an audit.

Do not attempt to delete or obscure the record. Access records cannot be edited, and the
attempt is itself recorded.

## Can I discuss evidence content with colleagues?

Only with colleagues working the same case, and only inside the platform. Not in general
channels, not in tickets that others can read, and not outside the platform at all.

Summarise the decision, not the content, in anything that a wider audience will see.

## What about verification documents?

The same principles, with the same recording. Review in the platform, do not retain copies,
access only for a review you are carrying out.

## A colleague has asked me to look at evidence for them

Decline, and tell them to request their own access. Access is attributed to the person who
opened it, for a reason recorded against their name.

Opening evidence on someone else's behalf makes the record wrong about who saw what.

## What if law enforcement requests evidence?

Do not provide it and do not confirm what exists. Route the request to the legal escalation
path immediately.

The procedure is in the internal operations handbook. Handling such a request informally
can compromise both a legal process and the platform's position.
