# Quoting

## What goes into a quote?

Your price and currency, an estimated duration, the scope you are proposing, the
deliverables, your assumptions, your exclusions, an expiry date, and the cancellation
terms.

Every one of those fields does work in a dispute. The quote is the agreement — what is not
in it was not agreed.

## How should I write the scope?

Describe what you will do, where, and what the customer receives. Be concrete enough that
someone reading it months later can tell whether you did it.

"Surveillance of the subject" is not a scope. "Up to twelve hours of observation at the
declared business address across three days, with a written report and time-stamped
photographs" is.

## Why do assumptions and exclusions matter so much?

They are your protection against a scope that quietly expands. Assumptions state what you
are relying on — that an address is current, that a subject is in the country, that a
document will be provided. Exclusions state what you are not doing.

Most disputes are about something the customer assumed was included. Writing exclusions
feels pessimistic and saves you repeatedly.

## How should I price?

However you judge the work to be worth. The platform does not set rates, suggest prices, or
publish what others charge.

Price the scope you wrote, not the scope you hope it turns out to be. Fees and taxes are
shown to the customer separately from your price — your quote is what your work is worth.

## What expiry should I set?

Long enough for the customer to decide, short enough that you are not held to a price and a
timeframe you can no longer meet.

Once a quote expires it cannot be accepted or reinstated. If a customer comes back after
expiry, submit a new quote — which is also an opportunity to reprice if circumstances
changed.

## Can I change a quote after submitting it?

Not once a customer has accepted it — that is the agreement. Before acceptance, withdraw it
and submit a replacement.

If the customer's needs change mid-assignment, that is a new quote for the additional work,
not an amendment to the existing one.

## Can I quote on several missions at once?

Yes. A quote is an offer, not a commitment of your calendar, until it is accepted.

Be careful with availability: if several outstanding quotes were all accepted, you should be
able to deliver on them. Overcommitting produces late work, which is what your rating
measures.

## What happens when a customer accepts?

Payment is taken, and once it is authorized the assignment is created and you are notified.
At that point you are committed to the scope and price you quoted.

You have a window to accept the assignment. Failing to accept it releases the customer and
counts against your response record.

## Can I negotiate with a customer?

You can discuss scope through the mission before quoting, and adjust what you propose
accordingly. What becomes binding is the quote, not the conversation.

If you agree something in messages, put it in the quote before the customer accepts.
