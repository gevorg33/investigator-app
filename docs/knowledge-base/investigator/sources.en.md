# Recording your sources

## What is a source, and why record one?

A source is where information in an assignment came from: a company register, a court record, a
website, a witness, a document the customer gave you, something you observed. It is separate from
the evidence you obtain from it — a register is the source; the extract you saved is evidence.

Recording sources as you go means your report can say where each finding came from, and it gives
anyone reviewing the work — the customer, or staff in a dispute — a trail they can follow.

## What do I record about a source?

Its type, a short title, and where to find it again: a URL, a register number, a file reference.
Record when you consulted it too, because a register extract is only true as of a date.

You can also say how reliable you judge the source to be: high, medium or low. If you do, you must
give your reason. A source whose reliability you have not assessed stays **unknown**, which is the
honest default and needs no reason.

## Is reliability the same as how sure I am of a finding?

No. Reliability is about the source: an official register is generally more reliable than an
anonymous forum post. How sure you are of a particular statement is a separate question, answered
where the statement is made, in the report. A reliable source can still carry a wrong claim.

## Can the customer see my sources?

Only the ones you choose to share. Every source is private when you record it. You can mark a
source as shared, and the customer will then see it; you can make it private again, and it
disappears from their view.

Think before sharing a source that names a person — a witness, a neighbour, a former colleague.
Sharing it tells the customer who they are. Keeping it private does not stop a finding in your
report from relying on it.

## Can I change or remove a source?

You can correct a source while the assignment is in progress, or after you have submitted your
report and are revising it. You cannot delete one, because your report or evidence may already rely
on it. You can **withdraw** it instead: a withdrawn source is taken out of use and out of the
customer's view, but it is kept, and a withdrawal cannot be undone.

## Why can I not add sources to this assignment?

Sources can be added, corrected and withdrawn only while the work is live — after you have
accepted the assignment, and until it is completed. Before you accept, the work is not yet yours;
once it is completed, cancelled or suspended, the sources are part of the record and are read-only.

## Can I reuse a source from another assignment?

No. Each assignment's sources belong to that assignment alone, and you record a source again when
you use it again. Sharing sources across assignments would reveal to one customer what another had
asked you to investigate.

## Does the platform visit the links I record?

No. A locator is a note of where you looked. The platform never opens or fetches it.
