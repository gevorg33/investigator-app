# Payouts

## When do I get paid?

The customer pays up front, when they accept your quote. That money is held rather than
passed straight to you, and is released through the payout process once the assignment
reaches completion.

Paying up front protects you: you are not chasing an invoice after the work is done. Holding
it until completion protects the customer. Both are why the platform sits in the middle.

## Why is the money not released as soon as I submit my report?

Because the customer has a review window in which they can accept the report or request
revisions. The assignment completes when that resolves.

An assignment left in customer review does not pay out. If a customer has gone quiet, prompt
them through the assignment and contact support if there is still no response.

## What is deducted from my price?

The platform fee, and any taxes applicable to your situation. Your earnings breakdown shows
each component separately for every assignment.

The price you quote is your price for the work. Fees are shown to the customer on top of it
rather than taken out of it — but check your breakdown, because how tax applies depends on
where you are.

## Where do I see what I am owed?

In your earnings view: what is held against in-progress assignments, what has completed and
is due, and what has been paid out, with the status of each.

Every figure traces to a specific assignment. If something does not reconcile, raise it with
the assignment reference rather than as a general query.

## Why has my payout not arrived?

Usually one of: the assignment has not completed, your payout details are incomplete or have
failed validation, or the payout is in transit with your bank.

Check the assignment status and your payout details first. Transfers take time on the
banking side that the platform does not control. If the status says paid and nothing has
arrived after a reasonable period, contact support with the payout reference.

## What happens if the customer gets a refund?

It depends on why. A refund under the quote's cancellation terms is calculated against the
work actually done — which is precisely why writing clear scope and cancellation terms
matters when you quote.

A refund ordered after a dispute reflects what staff found. If the dispute went against you,
the refund comes out of what you would have been paid.

## What is a chargeback and what happens to me?

A chargeback is a customer reversing a payment through their bank rather than through the
platform. It can happen after you have been paid.

The platform contests chargebacks where the record supports the work being delivered. Your
assignment record — the agreed scope, the messages, the evidence, the signed report — is
what that contest rests on. This is the concrete reason to keep everything on-platform.

## Can I ask a customer to pay me directly?

No. Payment outside the platform is outside the protections: no dispute route, no
chargeback defence, no record that the work was agreed.

It is also grounds for suspension. If a customer proposes it, decline and report it.

## Do I need to handle my own tax?

Yes. You are an independent professional, not an employee. The platform reports what it is
required to report in the jurisdictions it operates in, and your earnings records are
available for your own accounting.

What you owe and to whom is between you and your tax authority. Take your own advice on it.
