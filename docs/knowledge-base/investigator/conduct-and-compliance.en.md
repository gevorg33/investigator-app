# Conduct and compliance

## Who is responsible for the lawfulness of the work?

You are. The platform screens missions and verifies your documents, but it does not carry
your professional or legal obligations.

Neither a customer's instruction nor an accepted payment changes your legal position. If the
work would be unlawful where it happens, it is unlawful, and you are the one who did it.

## What should I do if a mission asks for something unlawful?

Do not quote on it, and report it. Do not quote a narrowed version hoping the customer
accepts the lawful part — that leaves you in a negotiation about how much of an unlawful
request you will fulfil.

Reporting is how patterns get caught. A single refused mission tells the platform nothing; a
pattern across several investigators does.

## A customer is pressuring me to go beyond what is lawful

Decline in writing, through the assignment, so there is a record. State what you will not do
and why.

If the pressure continues, report it and contact support. Do not handle it by quietly
delivering less than asked — an unexplained shortfall reads as non-delivery in a dispute,
whereas a documented refusal reads as professional judgement.

## What if the lawful position is unclear?

Take your own advice. The platform does not provide legal advice, and neither the assistant
nor support can tell you whether a particular act is lawful in your jurisdiction.

If you are unsure, the safe path is to decline and say why. A mission you did not take costs
you one fee. A mission you should not have taken can cost your licence.

## Does my licence cover everything I declared?

That is for you to confirm. Verification checks that the documents you supplied are valid
and cover what you declared; it does not independently research every specialty against
every jurisdiction's rules.

Declaring a specialty or a service area your licence does not extend to is a compliance
problem that surfaces at the worst possible moment.

## What happens to work in progress if my verification lapses?

Assignments already under way are not cancelled, but you stop appearing in discovery and
cannot quote until it is restored.

Restore it promptly. Continuing to work while a required licence has lapsed is a problem
between you and your regulator that the platform cannot resolve for you.

## What can get my account suspended?

Working outside what you are licensed for, taking payment off-platform, sharing account
credentials, abandoning assignments, mishandling evidence, abusive conduct toward customers,
or accepting work you should have refused.

Suspension decisions are recorded with their reason and you are told what it is. Where the
matter is serious, the relevant regulator or authority may also be informed.

## What can get me permanently banned?

Substantiated violation of professional or ethical obligations. Specifically: performing an
investigation improperly or negligently, deceiving or misleading a client, accepting an
assignment and abandoning it without valid reason, delivering work that fails the agreed scope,
or disclosing confidential information about a client, investigation or assignment to a
competitor or any unauthorised third party.

A ban is reserved for conduct that makes continued participation untenable. It is not the
ordinary outcome of a single dispute decided against you.

## How is a ban decided? Do I get to respond?

Yes. Before a permanent ban you are told what is alleged and on what basis, you get a
reasonable opportunity to respond, and the decision is made by someone who was not involved in
the underlying complaint. The decision and its reasoning are recorded and given to you, and you
can appeal to a different reviewer.

Your account may be suspended immediately as a precaution where leaving it active would risk
customers or their data. That is a suspension, not a ban, and the full process still follows.

## What is the difference between inadequate work and an honest limitation?

Inadequate work is measured against the scope in the quote you were accepted on. An
investigation that honestly reports it could not establish something has performed the work —
that is a finding, not a failure, and the limitations section exists for it.

This is why recording limitations as you encounter them matters. A limitation stated in the
report is a finding. The same limitation raised for the first time during a complaint is worth
much less.

## If I am banned, do I still get paid for work I completed?

A ban is not a financial penalty. Amounts earned for work already delivered remain payable.

Where a customer is owed a refund, that is decided separately on its own merits and may be set
against what you are owed. The two decisions are recorded separately.

## What happens to my live assignments if I am banned or suspended?

They are resolved as part of the action — reassigned, completed, or refunded — so customers are
not left without recourse. Evidence you already uploaded stays with the assignment; it belongs
to the case.

## Can I be removed for one mistake?

Most issues are handled proportionately — a warning, a correction, a conversation. The
platform's interest is in competent investigators staying.

The exceptions are the ones that put customers or their data at risk: unlawful work,
evidence mishandling, and taking payment off-platform. Those can end an account on a single
instance.

## What if I make an error in delivered work?

Say so, promptly, and correct it. Submit a corrected evidence item or a new report version
explaining what changed and why.

A self-reported and corrected error is a professional acting properly. The same error found
by a customer in a dispute is a different matter entirely.
