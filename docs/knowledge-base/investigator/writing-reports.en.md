# Writing the report

## What must the report contain?

A summary, the findings, the methodology you used, the limitations of what could be
determined, and the supporting attachments. You sign it before it is released.

The structure is not bureaucracy. A report that may be read in a dispute — or by a court —
needs to show not only what you found but how, and what you could not establish.

## How should I state a finding?

Tie it to evidence, and be clear about what kind of statement it is. A fact you can
evidence, a claim someone made to you, and an inference you drew are three different
things, and a reader must be able to tell them apart.

"The subject was observed leaving the address at 08:12 (photograph E-14)" is a fact.
"The subject appears to be living at the address" is an inference from several facts. State
the inference as an inference, with what it rests on.

## Why does the limitations section matter?

Because it is what makes the rest of the report credible. A report with no limitations reads
as either incurious or overreaching.

State what you could not determine and why — access you could not lawfully obtain, a period
you could not cover, a record that did not exist. Customers are told that limitations are a
sign of an honest report.

## What if my evidence contradicts itself?

Report the contradiction. Two sources giving different dates for the same event is a
finding, not a problem to tidy away.

Present both positions with their sources, say which if either you consider more reliable
and why, and state what would resolve it. Silently picking one and presenting it as settled
is the failure mode this platform is built to prevent.

## Can I use the AI assistant to help write my report?

For drafting structure and prose, yes. It can help you organise a methodology section or
tighten a summary.

It cannot originate a finding, assign a confidence, resolve a contradiction, or characterise
anything legally. Text it drafts is marked as AI-generated and unreviewed in the system, and
cannot be released to a customer until you have reviewed and approved it.

## Why is AI-drafted text marked even after I approve it?

Because approval records that you reviewed it; it does not erase how it originated. Both
facts are kept.

That is protective. If a report is later scrutinised, the record shows what was drafted with
assistance and that a qualified human approved it — which is a stronger position than an
unclear provenance.

## Can I change a report after submitting it?

Submitting creates a version. Revisions create new versions with an author, a timestamp and
a reason; earlier versions are retained.

Nothing is overwritten. The customer can see which version they are reading, and a dispute
can establish what was said at the time.

## The customer has requested a revision I disagree with

Respond in the revision rather than rewriting to satisfy it. If they are asking you to state
something the evidence does not support, say so and explain why.

You are not obliged to assert a conclusion you cannot evidence. A report that says "this
could not be established" and explains why is defensible; one that overreaches because a
customer wanted it to is not.

## Should I make a legal determination?

No. You report what you found. Whether that constitutes fraud, a breach, or grounds for
anything is a question for the customer's lawyers.

Describe conduct and evidence, not legal conclusions.
