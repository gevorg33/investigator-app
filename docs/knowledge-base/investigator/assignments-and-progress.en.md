# Running an assignment

## What happens when an assignment is created?

The customer has accepted your quote and paid. You are notified and have a window to accept
the assignment formally, which is what commits you to the work.

Until you accept, the work is not yours. After you accept, the scope, price and deadlines
in your quote are binding.

## What if I can no longer take the work?

Decline before accepting, and do it promptly — the customer can then go to someone else
while their timeframe still allows it.

If circumstances change after you have accepted, tell the customer immediately through the
assignment. Cancelling after acceptance is governed by your own cancellation terms and
affects your record.

## How much should I update the customer?

Enough that they are not wondering. Update when you start, when something material happens,
and when you are close to delivering.

Investigation has quiet stretches, and customers generally understand that — provided they
were told to expect them. Silence is what generates support contacts and disputes.

## The customer is asking for work outside the agreed scope

Say so, clearly and without treating it as a conflict. Most customers are not trying to get
free work; they did not realise the boundary.

Point to the exclusion or the scope line, and offer a new quote for the additional work.
Doing it for free "to keep them happy" sets a precedent and is not reflected in your rating.

## What if the work turns out to be impossible?

Tell the customer as soon as you know, and document why. An address that does not exist, a
subject who has left the jurisdiction, a record that is not obtainable lawfully — these are
findings, not failures.

Deliver what you have with a clear limitations section. Work honestly attempted and
honestly reported is defensible. Silence followed by nothing is not.

## What if the work would require something unlawful?

Stop. Tell the customer what you cannot do and why, and report the mission if the request
itself was unlawful.

Neither the customer's instruction nor the fact that you have been paid changes your legal
position. Completing unlawful work is your liability.

## Can I miss a deadline?

Avoid it, and if it is going to happen, say so before the deadline rather than after. A
deadline missed with notice is usually manageable; one missed silently is grounds for a
dispute.

Material delays change the value of the work to the customer, which is exactly what a
dispute assesses.

## Can I bring in a colleague to help?

The assignment is with you. You are responsible for the work delivered, for its lawfulness,
and for everything uploaded under your account.

Do not share your account credentials under any circumstances. If the work genuinely needs
another investigator, that is a separate mission for the customer to create.

## When is the assignment finished?

When you submit your report and the customer accepts it. They may request revisions first.

Completion is what moves the assignment toward payout. An assignment left in progress
because a final step was never taken does not pay out.
