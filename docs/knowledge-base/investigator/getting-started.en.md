# Working as an investigator

## What does the platform do for me?

It brings you missions from customers who have described what they need, handles the
agreement and the payment, and gives you the tools to deliver evidence and reports
securely.

You remain an independent professional. You set your own prices, decide which work to take,
define where you work and in what, and carry your own licensing and insurance obligations.

## Do I need a separate account to work as an investigator?

No. One account holds both the customer and the investigator role, and you switch between
them in the app.

The roles are separated for data access. Your investigator role cannot see your customer
missions, and the reverse also holds. This is deliberate — the same isolation applies to
you as to everyone else.

## What do I need before I can receive work?

A completed professional profile, verification documents submitted and approved, at least
one service area defined, and your specialties, services and languages declared.

Until verification is approved, you will not appear in customer discovery and cannot quote.
This is not a delay to work around — unverified investigators are excluded from results by
the system, not by convention.

## How do customers find me?

They describe what they need, and the platform matches missions to investigators by the
hard requirements: area covered, specialty, services offered, languages, availability and
verification status. Matches are then ordered by distance and quality signals.

An investigator who does not meet a hard requirement does not appear, regardless of how
well their profile text reads. Keeping your specialties, services and availability accurate
is what gets you seen.

## How do I actually get paid?

A customer accepts your quote and pays up front. The money is held and released to you
according to the payout process once the assignment reaches completion.

You are never asked to invoice the customer directly, and you should never accept payment
outside the platform. See the payouts article.

## What am I responsible for?

Working lawfully in the jurisdiction where the work happens, holding whatever licences that
jurisdiction requires, declining work you cannot lawfully or competently do, and delivering
what your quote said you would.

The platform verifies your documents and holds the agreement. It does not carry your legal
obligations for you.

## Can I work in more than one country?

Yes, if you are lawfully able to. Define a service area for each place you actually work,
and only declare specialties and services you can lawfully provide there.

Declaring an area you cannot lawfully work in produces missions you will have to decline,
which harms your response record.
